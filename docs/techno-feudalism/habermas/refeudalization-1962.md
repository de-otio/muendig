# Refeudalization — the 1962 Thesis

> See [README.md](README.md) for orientation. This file extracts the negative thesis of *Strukturwandel der Öffentlichkeit* (1962): how the bourgeois public sphere degrades, and Habermas's claim that what emerges is a *refeudalization* of publicness.

Habermas's negative thesis: from the late 19th century onward, the bourgeois public sphere ([bourgeois-public-sphere.md](bourgeois-public-sphere.md)) structurally degrades. The mechanisms he names:

- **Welfare-state blurring of public/private.** The state intervenes in the private sphere; private organisations (corporations, parties, lobbies) exercise public functions. The clean tripartite separation that the bourgeois public sphere depended on dissolves.
- **The press transforms from medium of debate to medium of consumption.** Mass-circulation papers funded by advertising sell readers to advertisers; the editorial relationship to readers shifts from "fellow participants in opinion-formation" to "audience to be retained."
- **Public relations and advertising replace argument.** Opinion is *manufactured* (Walter Lippmann, *Public Opinion*, 1922 — "the manufacture of consent"; the diagnostic is Habermas's). *Manipulative publicity* displaces *critical publicity*.
- **The public becomes audience.** Where the bourgeois public was a deliberating collective producing opinion, the mass public is a population having opinion produced for it.

## Representational publicness — the load-bearing concept

The most consequential single move in the book is Habermas's claim that what emerges in this transformation is a *refeudalization* of publicness. His argument:

Feudal "publicness" was *representational* — the lord representing himself before the assembled people (royal procession, court ritual, display of status, theatrical performance of authority). It was publicness *as display*, not publicness *as discourse*. The bourgeois public sphere displaced representational publicness with critical-rational publicness for roughly two centuries. Mass media, PR, and consumer advertising bring back representational publicness in modern dress: the corporation, the celebrity, the political figure performing themselves before an audience that consumes the performance rather than debating it.

This is *Habermas's word*, in 1962, in print. The techno-feudal argument inherits a diagnosis that already named itself.
