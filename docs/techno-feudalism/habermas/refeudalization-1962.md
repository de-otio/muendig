# Refeudalization — the 1962 Thesis

> See [README.md](README.md) for orientation. This file extracts the negative thesis of *Strukturwandel der Öffentlichkeit* (1962): how the bourgeois public sphere degrades, and Habermas's claim that what emerges is a *refeudalization* of publicness.

This is the negative thesis of *Strukturwandel* in its 1962 formulation. Habermas himself qualified several of these claims in the 1990 preface to the new German edition of the book (Suhrkamp, 1990) [`preface page ref to verify`] — the points below should be read as the 1962 argument, not the position Habermas held continuously through to the 2022 essay. See [further-transformation-2022.md](further-transformation-2022.md) for the digital-era update.

Habermas's negative thesis: from the late 19th century onward, the bourgeois public sphere ([bourgeois-public-sphere.md](bourgeois-public-sphere.md)) structurally degrades. The mechanisms he names (Burger trans. 1989, [`chs. V–VI / §§ ref to verify`]):

- **Welfare-state blurring of public/private.** The state intervenes in the private sphere; private organisations (corporations, parties, lobbies) exercise public functions. The clean tripartite separation that the bourgeois public sphere depended on dissolves.
- **The press transforms from medium of debate to medium of consumption.** Mass-circulation papers funded by advertising sell readers to advertisers; the editorial relationship to readers shifts from "fellow participants in opinion-formation" to "audience to be retained." (Burger trans. 1989, [`ch. V / §16 to verify`].)
- **Public relations and advertising replace argument.** Opinion is *manufactured* — Walter Lippmann uses the formulation "manufacture of consent" in *Public Opinion* (Harcourt, Brace, 1922), Ch. XV [`page ref to verify`]; the phrase's modern currency derives from Edward Herman and Noam Chomsky, *Manufacturing Consent: The Political Economy of the Mass Media* (Pantheon, 1988). The diagnostic that publicity has flipped from critical to manipulative — *manipulative Publizität* displacing *kritische Publizität* — is Habermas's [`§§18–20 / Burger ch. VI to verify`].
- **The public becomes audience.** Where the bourgeois public was a deliberating collective producing opinion, the mass public is a population having opinion produced for it.

## Representational publicness — the load-bearing concept

The most consequential single move in the book is Habermas's claim that what emerges in this transformation is a *Refeudalisierung* — a refeudalization — of publicness. His argument:

Feudal "publicness" was *repräsentative Öffentlichkeit* / *representational publicness* — the lord representing himself before the assembled people (royal procession, court ritual, display of status, theatrical performance of authority). It was, on Habermas's framing, publicness *as display* rather than publicness *as discourse*. The display/discourse contrast is itself a Habermasian binary; agonistic-pluralist readers (e.g. Chantal Mouffe; Michael Warner, *Publics and Counterpublics*, 2002 [`page ref to verify`]) resist the implication that display is necessarily anti-deliberative.

Habermas argues that the bourgeois public sphere displaced representational publicness with critical-rational publicness for roughly two centuries. (This historical claim is the one most heavily contested by the standard challenges — Fraser 1990 on constitutive exclusions, Eley 1992 on plebeian counterpublics, Landes 1988 on gender; see [critiques.md](critiques.md). The "two-centuries" trajectory is on the textbook reading; on the revisionist reading the bourgeois public sphere never enjoyed the universality the trajectory implies.) Mass media, PR, and consumer advertising, on Habermas's diagnosis, bring back representational publicness in modern dress: the corporation, the celebrity, the political figure performing themselves before an audience that consumes the performance rather than debating it.

*Refeudalisierung* is Habermas's term, in *Strukturwandel* (1962) [`§ / page ref to verify`]. The techno-feudal argument inherits a diagnosis that already named itself. See [mapping-to-techno-feudalism.md](mapping-to-techno-feudalism.md) for the direct connections to the platform case, and [further-transformation-2022.md](further-transformation-2022.md) for Habermas's own digital-era update.
