# Habermas — Notes on the Public Sphere

The single most important bridge text between the [Enlightenment lineage](../enlightenment-lineage.md) and the [techno-feudal critique](../README.md). Habermas does two things that are directly load-bearing here:

1. He gives a concrete, historically grounded account of what the Enlightenment's "public" actually was — the social and infrastructural formation in which Locke's, Kant's, and Mill's arguments could land. ([bourgeois-public-sphere.md](bourgeois-public-sphere.md))
2. He uses the word *refeudalization* (*Refeudalisierung*) for what happens when that public sphere decays under mass media. The diagnosis was made in 1962, before any of the current infrastructure existed; the techno-feudal argument is in significant part a continuation of his. ([refeudalization-1962.md](refeudalization-1962.md))

He also revisited the thesis explicitly for the digital era in a 2022 essay. Both texts are needed for an honest read.

## Why this matters here

The Enlightenment lineage gives the moral and political principles — autonomy, consent, individuality, freedom of expression. Habermas tells us *what those principles required as a social formation* and *what is being lost when that formation degrades*. The combined diagnosis is sharper than either alone: the techno-feudal arrangement erodes both the principles (Enlightenment) and the social conditions (Habermas) on which independent creative publication depends.
